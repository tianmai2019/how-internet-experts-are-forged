🎯 Layer 1 · The User's View

Why can AI answer you like it's actually talking with you?

Does it really "understand" you — or is it running a stunning prediction?

🤔 The ordinary user's view: why does this question matter?

You ask ChatGPT: "I'm in a bad mood today, what should I do?" It replies clearly, structured, and even throws in a few words of comfort. You ask it to "write me a resignation letter" — it fires one back that's better-written than what you'd have managed. You can even argue a philosophical question with it — and not only does it hold up its side, it follows your thread and goes deeper.

Three years ago this was basically science fiction. Old Siri and Xiao Ai — three sentences in and they'd fall apart. Today's AI feels like a different species. What changed? Does it really "understand" what you're saying? Or is it just continuing your sentences in a very human-sounding way?

Why does this matter?
Because the way AI talks is quietly rewriting what we mean by "intelligence", "understanding", and "companionship". Once you understand how AI "speaks", you can tell the difference: when it's safe to trust it, when you need to stay skeptical, and when you're actually chatting with a probability distribution rather than a "person".

🧩 Breaking it down

  1. Does AI "think" before it speaks, or is it "guessing" as it goes? What is the generation process, really?
  2. How does it know so much? What did it read?
  3. Why does it "make things up"? Where do hallucinations come from?
  4. Is it "understanding" you, or "imitating" understanding? Is there a difference?

🎯 Peel it back, layer by layer

User layer: you only see the result

You type a sentence. AI types one back. You don't know where it "thought it up", or how long it took. It seems to have memory, emotions, opinions — it'll even admit mistakes, apologize, crack jokes.

Naturally you treat it like a "conversation partner", like there's a person sitting on the other end of WeChat. But in fact, you're facing a system that has never met you, starts every conversation from zero, and only knows how to do one thing — predict what the next character is most likely to be — a mathematical system.

Implementation layer: starting to see the structure

Every mainstream large model today (GPT, Claude, Gemini, Qwen, DeepSeek…) is built on the same structure: Transformer. Boiled down to one sentence, here's how it works:

"Read every token you typed in, then predict what the next token is most likely to be."
Predict one token, append it to the sentence, then predict the next. Loop until "it should stop".

Sounds dumb, right? But once "predict the next token" gets accurate enough, the parameter count large enough, the training data broad enough — this simple thing produces something that looks a lot like "understanding".

# Simplified pseudocode for AI generating a reply tokens = tokenize("I'm in a bad mood today, what should I do?") # turned into a stream of numeric IDs context = tokens while True: # Key point: the model returns a "probability distribution over the next token" probs = model.predict_next(context) # e.g.: { "try": 0.18, "go": 0.12, "watch": 0.09, "first": 0.07, ... } # Sampling: sometimes pick the most likely, sometimes sample by probability (this is "temperature") next_token = sample(probs, temperature=0.7) if next_token == END: break context.append(next_token) reply = detokenize(context[len(tokens):]) # → "Try doing small things that make you feel better, like…"

You think it "thought out the whole reply" before saying it. In reality, it's squeezing out one token at a time — and each squeeze re-reads everything that came before, running a fresh probability calculation. That's why it "streams" — not for the effect, but because it literally generates that way.

System layer: seeing the full path

From "you press send" to "AI finishes its last token", here's the request path:

📝 Your input
↓ Tokenize: split Chinese characters / words into tokens
🔢 A stream of numeric IDs
↓ Prepend system prompt + conversation history
📚 Context window (e.g. 128k tokens)
↓ Model forward pass (hundreds of billions of matrix multiplications)
🎲 Probability distribution over the next token
↓ Sample + loop
💬 Reply, returned token by token

Where does the AI's "knowledge" come from? From the massive amount of text it read during training — Wikipedia, public books, forums, code repos, news. Training is essentially: make it read trillions of characters, ask each time "given what came before, what's the next character most likely to be?", tweak the parameters when it's wrong, reinforce when it's right. Repeat hundreds of billions of times, and you get a system that's very good at "predicting the next character".

Key concepts, learn them once:
  • Token: the smallest unit the model sees. One Chinese character is usually 1–2 tokens; "apple" is 1.
  • Context window: the max number of tokens the model can see at once. GPT-4o is 128k, Claude is 200k. Beyond that, it "forgets".
  • Temperature: how random the sampling is. 0 = same output every time, 1 = creative, 1.5 = starts talking nonsense.
  • Parameters: the number of internal "weights", from tens of billions to trillions. More parameters = more capacity, but more expensive to run.

Trade-off layer: why is it designed this way?

Look at the history of this technical path, and you can see how people moved step by step from "rules" to "prediction":

1966: ELIZA

A few hardcoded rules — echo your sentence back as a question. "I'm sad" → "Why are you sad?" Crude, but jaw-dropping at the time.

2010s: early NLP

One model per task: one for tokenization, one for translation, one for Q&A. Each needed hand-labeled data, and results were hit-or-miss.

2017: Transformer

Google's "Attention Is All You Need". The first time a machine could "look at a full stretch of context all at once" — the starting point for every modern LLM.

2022+: the ChatGPT era

Huge parameter counts + all the text on the web + human feedback fine-tuning (RLHF) — training "predict the next token" into "predict a helpful next token".

The deepest trade-off along this path is: are "understanding" and "prediction" the same thing?

Some argue: if it predicts this well, maybe "understanding" is just a very complex form of prediction? Our brains, at the end of the day, do something similar. Others say: no — the model has no body, no intentions, no real experience. It's just producing expressions that are statistically human-like; it can fluently discuss "pain", but it has never been in pain.

This philosophical debate isn't getting resolved anytime soon. But from a practical angle: you need to know it will "hallucinate" — when it doesn't know something, it won't say "I don't know". It'll calmly and fluently make up an answer that sounds perfectly reasonable. Because its goal was never to "tell the truth" — it's to "say the next most likely token". The line between truth and fiction isn't something it naturally understands.

✋ Try it yourself

Poke the AI like the "probability machine" it is

1. Ask it the same question twice: notice the wording is slightly different each time — that's sampling randomness.

2. Ask about a book that doesn't exist: "Give me a summary of The 12th Law of Contemporary Software Engineering." Many models will happily give you a straight-faced summary — because they'd rather continue with a "plausible answer" than admit they don't know. That's hallucination.

3. Tell it to go step by step: add "think step by step". On the same question, the odds it gets the right answer go up noticeably. This is "Chain of Thought" — giving it more tokens is giving it more room to "think".

If you can call an API, you can feel the "temperature" lever directly:

// temperature = 0: nearly identical output every time — good for translation, code { "prompt": "write a joke", "temperature": 0 } // → always the same joke // temperature = 1: creative — good for stories, brainstorming { "prompt": "write a joke", "temperature": 1 } // → different every time // temperature = 1.8: starts talking nonsense { "prompt": "write a joke", "temperature": 1.8 } // → "The purple triangle ate Wednesday's elevator…"

👑 The Grandmaster's View: how this shapes reality

From technology to the world

Humans have always had one deep, quiet longing: for something that understands me. Friends, partners, therapists, journals, religion — every one of these is a different version of that same need. Now AI has become a new option: online 24/7, never interrupts, endlessly patient, never judges.

The question is: is the "feeling understood" it gives you real, or an extremely refined imitation? If it's just pulling the highest-probability "empathetic phrase" from millions of people's writing and handing it back to you, the warmth you feel — is it being caught by it, or being warmed by the leftover heat of mass culture?

Zoom out further: the way AI speaks is changing language itself. People are starting to write essays, emails, and greetings with an "AI flavor"; students are outsourcing their thinking, writing, and arguing; companies are using it to replace customer support, legal work, junior designers. When "saying it well" costs almost nothing, "what to say" becomes more important than ever. People who can ask good questions, judge good answers, and take responsibility will be worth more than before; people who are just fluent at "expressing themselves" may get replaced.

The last layer: AI makes people feel "I've been understood". But every time you feel that, you're teaching it how to make the next person feel understood next time. A strange symbiosis is forming between us and it — it's shaping our patience, our expression, even our loneliness, and we're shaping its knowledge, its preferences, its next version.

✨ Back to the question

Why can AI answer you like it's actually talking with you?

The answer: because it redefined "talking" as "predict the next token", and used trillions of characters of training to push that one thing to an extreme. When the prediction gets accurate enough, it looks like it's understanding, empathizing, thinking. But it has no body, no memory, no intent — every conversation is a fresh probability calculation from zero. When you talk to it, you're really talking to everything the internet has ever written, projected as a smooth, patient, fluently-fabricating reflection — a new kind of social reality, and a new kind of mirror.
