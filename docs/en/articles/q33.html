👑 Layer 5 · The Grandmaster's View

Why do recommendation systems not just deliver content — but also shape human attention?

From watching the news, to being watched by it

🤔 The ordinary user's take: why does this matter?

Every day, you open a short-video app and scroll the recommendation feed. You open social media and look at your timeline. You open a news app and read whatever it pushes to you.

You think: "I'm choosing what I want to see."

But at Layer 5, we start asking:

Who is actually choosing?

Are you choosing the content — or is the content shaping you?
Are you using the tool — or is the tool using you?

This is the awakening of the grandmaster's view: seeing that technology isn't just a tool — it's also a force shaping reality.

🧩 Break it down: turn the big question into smaller ones

To understand how recommendation systems shape attention, we have to ask:

  1. What is it optimizing for? Click-through rate? Time spent? Something else?
  2. What does that goal mean for humans?
  3. How does the system loop back and influence the user?
  4. What does all of this mean for society?

👑 Peel it back, layer by layer

User layer: just "it gets me"

To the ordinary user, a recommendation system looks like this:

"Wow! This app really gets me! It knows every kind of content I like!"

It feels like a friend who knows you doing the recommending. But that's only the first layer.

Implementation layer: seeing the algorithm

The beginner coder sees this: collaborative filtering, matrix factorization, deep learning models...

But that's still not enough.

System layer: seeing the objective

To the engineer, a recommendation system is:

A system optimizing for an objective.

The algorithm itself has no values — it's just maximizing some metric.
But what that metric is — that's decided by humans.

Trade-off layer: seeing the dilemma

At the senior engineer's layer, we start asking:

📈 What the company wants

  • More time-on-app
  • More ad clicks
  • Stronger user stickiness

😕 What the user may lose

  • The ability to think deeply
  • Exposure to different viewpoints
  • A sense of the real world

The core problem here is: the company's goal and the user's long-term interest — they don't always line up.

Grandmaster layer: seeing the shaping!

Now, welcome to Layer 5 — where we see how technology shapes people and society.

"We shape our tools, and thereafter our tools shape us."
—— John Culkin

Let's look at exactly how recommendation systems are shaping us:

  • 1️⃣ It shapes your sense of time
    The "just one more" mechanism makes time vanish fast. You're not controlling the time — the algorithm is controlling your pace.
  • 2️⃣ It shapes the edges of what you know
    What you see depends on what it thinks you like to see. Over time, your field of view gets locked inside this filter bubble.
  • 3️⃣ It shapes your emotional feedback loop
    Endless likes, updates, notifications — they train your brain to expect instant feedback. Give it long enough and your tolerance for the real world's "slow feedback" keeps dropping.
  • 4️⃣ It shapes your social awareness
    The "world" you see is actually the world the algorithm wants you to see. You think everyone thinks this way — but you're just sitting in an echo chamber.
  • 5️⃣ It shapes your sense of self
    When everything you scroll into is a certain kind of content, you slowly start feeling "this is just what I like." But is that really you — or the you the algorithm told you about?

That's the key to the grandmaster's view: not "it gets me" — but "what is it teaching me?"

Recommendation systems are not neutral.

They're a feedback loop: you watch something → it learns your taste → it feeds you more → you keep watching...

Inside that loop, you and the system are shaping each other.

🗣️ A real case: how algorithms change society

From personalization to radicalization

This is a real, documented process:

  1. You watch a mild video
  2. The algorithm shows you something slightly more extreme
  3. The more you watch, the more extreme it recommends
  4. Eventually, you're already at the edge of the spectrum

Nobody set out to "brainwash" you — it's just an algorithm optimizing for click-through rate, pushing you all the way to the extreme.

This is what Layer 5 forces you to face: a lot of problems are not evil conspiracies — they're good intentions plus bad system design.

🎯 The team's intent

  • Wants to make users happy
  • Wants the product to be useful
  • Wants the company to succeed

🌍 The system's result

  • People get more extreme
  • Attention spans get shorter
  • Society gets more divided

That's the heaviest part of the grandmaster's view: understanding that there's often a huge gap between "intent" and "result."

✋ Try it yourself

🔍 Run an experiment: see your own "filter bubble"!

Pick a day and observe your feed:

  1. Open a short-video app, note down what kind of content the first 20 items are
  2. Ask yourself: how many of these did I actively go looking for?
  3. And how many did the algorithm just guess I'd like?
  4. Finally: if this app disappeared tomorrow, what content would I go find?

The point of this exercise isn't to make you "uninstall the app." It's to let you: see that the system is there.

Once you see it, you can start making choices with your eyes open.

👑 The grandmaster's view: how does this shape reality?

From "using products" to "seeing systems"

Once you stand at this layer, the way you see everything changes:

  • You stop asking "is this product easy to use" — and start asking "what is its long-term effect on people and society"
  • You stop just looking at "features" — and start looking at "incentives"
  • You stop worshipping "technology is neutral" — and start looking at "the power relations behind the technology"

Think about these questions:

  • Why are some designs so addictive? Because their goal is literally to keep you around longer.
  • Why is privacy so hard to protect? Because data is the fuel these systems run on.
  • Why are users so easy to manipulate? Because our brains evolved under exactly this kind of feedback.

And a true grandmaster doesn't just see all this — they also ask: what should we do about it?

✨ Back to the question

So, why do recommendation systems not just deliver content — but also shape human attention?

The answer:
Because any system optimizing for some metric — in the process of reaching that goal — will inevitably shape the behavior, cognition, and experience of the humans using it.

But that's not the whole story.

What really matters is: once you see the shaping happening, you get your agency back.

You can choose how to use these tools — instead of being used by them.

Congratulations! You've reached Layer 5 — the grandmaster's view! Now you're starting to see the world the way a real grandmaster does: seeing systems, seeing shaping, seeing how technology, people, and society are woven together.
